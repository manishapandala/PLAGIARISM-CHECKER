U.S. public safety is controversial because debates about crime policy often use the same numbers but emphasize different periods. One narrative says the country is significantly safer than in the early 1990s. The opposing narrative argues that the recent jump in lethal violence proves danger remains elevated.

Both visualizations below use the same source dataset: Our World in Data - Homicide rate (UNODC) . The opposing arguments are produced through framing choices: time-window selection, axis design, annotation style, and color connotation.

Left chart rhetoric writeup (affirmative framing)

I designed the left chart to persuade viewers that progress is real. The primary technique is temporal framing: using the full 1990-2023 horizon changes the story from short-term volatility to long-term decline. I also use scale framing with a y-axis that starts at zero, which visually compresses yearly fluctuations and strengthens the impression of steady improvement.

The cool blue palette and "recovery" language are deliberate affective cues that reduce urgency. Endpoint and peak annotations create a memorable before/after comparison, making the argument persuasive by showing that present conditions look better than the high-violence era that many people remember.

Right chart rhetoric writeup (negative framing)

I designed the right chart to persuade viewers that concern is still justified. The key technique is selective windowing: by isolating 2019-2023, the sudden escalation appears central rather than incidental. I also use axis truncation (starting at 4.5 instead of zero), which amplifies the visual steepness of the increase.

Warm reds, warning labels, and explicit baseline comparison are narrative urgency cues. These choices frame the same data as a warning story: despite long-run gains, recent rates remain elevated enough to support a skeptical "not safe enough" viewpoint.
