Topic: Is the United States becoming safer from lethal violence?

Homicide trends are politically and socially controversial because different groups focus on different time windows to argue opposite claims about safety, public policy, and social progress. One side emphasizes long-term decline compared with the early 1990s, while the other side emphasizes the sharp rise around 2020 and argues that danger remains high.

Both charts below use the same source dataset: Our World in Data - Homicide rate (UNODC) . The framing changes through selective time windows, baseline choices, and color/annotation emphasis.

Yes: America is much safer than in the 1990s

This chart highlights the full 1990-2023 trend and emphasizes the large long-run decline from about 9.6 to 5.8 homicides per 100,000.

No: Recent years still show a public-safety warning

This chart zooms into 2019-2023 and uses a truncated y-axis to stress the abrupt jump in 2020-2021 and the still-elevated 2023 value relative to 2019.

Left chart rhetoric writeup (affirmative framing)

I use temporal framing by showing the entire 1990-2023 range, which makes the dominant narrative a long-term decline. I also use color connotation (cool blue/green) and a zero-baseline y-axis, both of which reduce perceived volatility and visually communicate gradual improvement. Finally, the annotation compares endpoints to reinforce the idea that current conditions are much better than the historic peak period.

Right chart rhetoric writeup (negative framing)

I use selective windowing to isolate 2019-2023, where the most dramatic increase is visible. I also apply axis truncation (starting near 4.5 instead of zero), which magnifies year-to-year differences and makes the 2020-2021 surge look alarmingly steep. Warm red tones and callout labels increase urgency, supporting a skeptical argument that recent trends still justify concern despite any long-term progress.
